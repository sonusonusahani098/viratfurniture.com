Nobody notices a good bed. That's its job — eight silent hours, no participation in your turning over, no commentary. Which is why a failing bed is so uniquely maddening: the creak that speaks every time you move, the corner that dips, the storage lid that stopped lifting, the slat that cracks mid-sleep like a starting pistol. You spend a third of your life on this furniture; it should not have opinions.

Bed repair is steady work at our Surat workshop — partly because beds take real structural load daily, and partly because so many were built lighter than their job demands.

## The anatomy of bed failure

A bed is a frame doing structural duty. Failures cluster predictably:

**Joints** — the corner and rail joints loosen from years of cyclic load. The fix is proper re-gluing, re-blocking and hardware reinforcement, not a squirt of oil and hope.

**Slats and supports** — the platform under the mattress. Broken slats almost always signal an under-built platform: too thin, too few, or missing a centre rail. We fix the cause, not just the casualty.

**Headboards** — loosened fixings, cracked panels, tired upholstery. Re-fixed, re-built or re-upholstered as needed.

**Storage mechanisms** — box-storage lids off their hinges and hydraulic lifts that no longer lift. Pistons get replaced with units rated for your actual mattress weight, and the mounting points — usually the true failure — get reinforced.

**Legs and levelling** — split legs, crushed corners, and the wobble that comes from a frame no longer square. Squared, braced, silenced.

The free doorstep inspection reads the whole frame, not just the complaint — a creak in one corner often has a cousin loosening quietly in another.
