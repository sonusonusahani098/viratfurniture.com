Solid wood doesn't wear out — its finish does. The dining table that looks exhausted, the doors gone cloudy around the handles, the sheesham chairs that have lost their glow: underneath every one of them, the wood is usually fine, waiting under a spent layer of old polish. Refinishing removes that layer and rebuilds it, and the transformation is the closest thing furniture work has to magic — the piece doesn't look repaired; it looks like its own younger self.

Polishing has been part of our Surat workshop for two decades, from daily-use dining sets to inherited furniture carrying three generations of family history.

## What refinishing actually involves

Real polishing is a process, not a wipe-on product. The failed finish comes off — stripped or sanded back to honest wood. Damage gets addressed while the surface is open: dents filled, scratches levelled, water stains bleached or blended, colour evened where sun has faded one side (the window-side fade every Surat home knows). Stain goes on next if the tone is changing or deepening. Then the new finish builds in coats — sprayed or hand-applied, with proper drying between — until the surface has the depth people mistake for expensive new furniture.

Cutting corners at any stage shows within months, which is why quick-visit "polish walas" so often disappoint by Diwali. Done properly, a refinish lasts years of daily use.

## Choosing the right finish, honestly

- **Melamine** — the sensible default: warm sheen, fair durability, friendly cost. Most bedroom and living furniture lives happily here.
- **PU (polyurethane)** — the tough one: superior resistance to heat, moisture and daily abuse, with a richer build. Our standing recommendation for dining tables and heavy-duty surfaces; costlier, and worth it exactly there.
- **Traditional spirit polish** — for antiques and heritage pieces, the authentic hand-rubbed glow that suits their age. Slower, softer, and the only respectful choice on some furniture.
- **Matte to high gloss** — sheen level is a style call; current taste runs matte-to-satin, but your gloss is your gloss.

We recommend per piece, explain costs plainly, and sample any colour change on a hidden corner before the whole piece commits.
