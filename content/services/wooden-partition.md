Almost every Surat flat built in the last decade shares one layout quirk: the front door opens directly into the living room. Guests, delivery boys and the whole hall greet each other at once. The traditional fix — a masonry wall — steals light and can't be undone. The better fix, most of the time, is a wooden partition: enough separation to give the room its privacy back, without giving up the openness the layout was bought for.

Partition work has become a steady part of what leaves our Surat workshop — entry screens, kitchen-dining dividers, study separations and office layouts, each designed for its exact spot.

## The louvre effect

The design most clients settle on, once they see it, is the vertical louvre or slat screen — evenly spaced wooden battens running floor to ceiling. It breaks the direct sightline and defines the entry, but light and air pass through, so the hall stays bright and the flat doesn't shrink. Louvres also happen to be the most photographed partition style in Indian interiors right now, and for once the trend is genuinely practical.

Variations we build regularly: slats over a low storage base (the entry shoe cabinet problem solved in the same footprint), slats with a planter groove at the bottom, back-to-back designs finished on both faces, and fluted-glass-with-wood combinations where a touch more separation is wanted without losing brightness.

## Dividers that store, screens that work

A partition occupies real floor area, so we like it to work for a living:

- **Entry partitions** with shoe storage below and display or mandir niche above
- **Kitchen-dining dividers** with a serving counter or crockery unit built into the divider itself
- **Study screens** — a bookshelf-partition hybrid that walls off a work-from-home corner and holds its books
- **Pooja separations** that give the mandir its own defined, respectful space in an open plan — often paired with a full [pooja unit](/services/pooja-unit)
- **Office partitions** separating reception, cabins and work zones, often combined with our [office furniture](/services/office-furniture) fit-outs

## Materials and detailing

Slats and frames come in solid wood or ply with veneer/laminate finish depending on budget — the difference matters at the edges and in how the polish takes. Bases and storage sections follow our usual carcass standards in ISI-grade plywood. Anchoring is engineered to the site: floor-fixed, ceiling-braced or wall-returned as the location demands, and always done to be reversible with minimal scarring. For glass elements we use toughened or fluted panels set into wood framing.

Finish-wise, most partitions take PU polish or laminate in wood tones matched to your other furniture — which is why partition projects often travel with [wall panelling](/services/wall-panel) or a [wooden ceiling](/services/wooden-ceiling) element in the same palette.

## Straightforward process, quick timeline

Partitions are among our fastest projects: a free site visit and measurement, a design conversation about openness versus separation, an itemised quote, then manufacture and installation — typically 7-10 days end to end for a single screen. Installation is usually done in a day, with minimal mess since all cutting and finishing has happened at the workshop.
