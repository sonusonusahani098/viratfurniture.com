Restaurants are theatres that serve food, and the furniture is the set design — it tells guests what the meal will cost and how long they're welcome to linger before the menu ever arrives. It also gets treated worse than furniture anywhere else: sauce, steam, hourly wipe-downs, chairs dragged a hundred times a service. Getting both halves right — the look and the endurance — is the entire craft.

Our Surat workshop builds for restaurants, cafes, and food businesses across the city — new fit-outs and the refreshes that keep a dining room feeling current.

## Seating that sets the room's rhythm

The seating mix decides how a restaurant feels and how many covers it turns:

- **Booth and banquette seating** along walls — the most requested format for good reason: it seats more people per running foot, feels premium, and defines the room's edges. We build the upholstered benches, backs and the panelling above as one composition.
- **Freestanding tables and chairs** for the flexible middle floor — two-tops that join for families, chairs built to be dragged (glides fitted, joints braced).
- **Community and window-bar seating** for cafes — long shared tables and counter-height rails where laptops and cold coffee settle in.
- **Waiting benches** at the door, because a Friday-night queue given somewhere to sit is a queue that stays.

Comfort is calibrated to your service style — QSR seating politely encourages turnover; family dining cushions settle guests in for the full thali.

## Surfaces chosen for the mop and the years

Restaurant materials are chosen defensively. Tabletops take compact laminate or hard-sealed wood that tolerates hot plates and constant wiping; booth upholstery is leatherette or treated fabric that cleans between seatings; edges everywhere are impact-banded because trays, chairs and belt buckles find them relentlessly. Under it all, ISI-grade carcass work and branded hardware — a wobbling table is the fastest way to lose a table's tip, and a broken booth loses its covers all week.
