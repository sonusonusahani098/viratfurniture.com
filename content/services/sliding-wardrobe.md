There's a particular bedroom layout you see all over Surat's newer apartments: the bed along one wall, the wardrobe wall barely three feet away, and hinged wardrobe doors that clash with the bed frame every time they open. Sliding wardrobes exist for exactly this room. The doors glide sideways on tracks instead of swinging out, and suddenly the same floor plan breathes.

We've been building sliding wardrobes at our Surat workshop for years — from single-bedroom retrofits to full-tower builder packages — and the difference between one that delights and one that irritates comes down to two things: honest carcass construction and hardware that can actually carry the doors.

## Why the hardware matters more than the shutter

A sliding shutter is heavy — a full-height panel with laminate on both faces, or worse, a mirror, weighs far more than a hinged door. All of that weight rides on the track system every single day. This is where budget builds fail: undersized channels that start dragging within a year, doors that derail, that grinding sound every morning.

Our sliding wardrobes run on branded aluminium track systems matched to the shutter weight, with soft-close dampers available so doors settle shut instead of banging. The brand and model go into your written quotation. It's the least glamorous line in the quote and the one that decides how the wardrobe feels in year five.

## Shutter options that earn their keep

Sliding panels are large, flat and prominent — effectively a feature wall in the bedroom. Popular choices from our workshop:

- **Laminate panels** in wood grains or solids — the durable, low-maintenance default.
- **Mirror panels** — one mirrored shutter turns the wardrobe into the dressing area and visually doubles a small room.
- **Lacquered glass** for a premium, easy-to-clean gloss in contemporary interiors.
- **Combination fronts** — laminate framing with a mirror or glass strip inset, which breaks up the mass of a wide wardrobe nicely.

Inside, the same made-to-measure logic applies as our [hinged wooden wardrobes](/services/wooden-wardrobe): hanging sections planned around your clothes, shelves at the heights you actually use, drawers with locks where needed, and loft storage above if ceiling height allows.

## Honest guidance on when *not* to slide

We'd rather lose a hardware upgrade than fit the wrong wardrobe. Sliding doors have two genuine trade-offs: you can only open one section at a time, and very narrow openings make sliding panels impractical. If your wall is under six feet, or you're someone who likes the whole wardrobe open while choosing clothes, we'll say so and quote a hinged design instead. Plenty of our projects end up with sliding in the compact bedroom and hinged in the master — the right answer per room.
