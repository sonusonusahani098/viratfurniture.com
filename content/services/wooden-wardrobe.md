A wardrobe is the one piece of furniture you open every single day, and it's also the piece most likely to be bought wrong — too shallow for hangers, shelves in the wrong places, a gap above collecting dust because the unit stops three feet short of the ceiling. After building wardrobes for Surat homes for more than two decades, we've come to a simple conclusion: the wardrobe should be designed around your clothes and your wall, not picked from a catalogue.

## Built to your wall, floor to ceiling

Most of the wardrobes we make are wall-to-wall and floor-to-ceiling, because in Surat's apartments that's simply the best use of the bedroom. The main wardrobe body sits at usable height, and a loft section above takes the things you touch twice a year — suitcases, winter bedding, festival decorations. No dead gap on top, no wasted strip on the side.

During measurement we note everything that affects the build: skirting height, switchboard positions, window clearance, ceiling level differences (more common than people think), and which way the room's traffic flows — which decides whether hinged doors even work for you or whether you should be looking at a [sliding wardrobe](/services/sliding-wardrobe) instead.

## Designed around how you store clothes

Before we design internals, we ask questions most furniture shops skip. Who uses this wardrobe? Mostly hanging or mostly folded? Sarees? Office wear that needs separation from home wear? Kids' section that they can actually reach? The answers shape the interior:

- Full-height hanging for kurtas and dresses, double-hung rods for shirts and trousers
- Deep shelves for folded stacks, shallow ones for daily-wear rotation
- Lockable drawers for documents and jewellery
- Pull-out trays, belt and tie organisers, and mirror panels where useful
- Loft sections sized to your actual suitcases, not a generic depth

This is the part that makes a custom wardrobe feel custom six months in — not the shutter colour, but the fact that nothing lives in an awkward spot.

## Material choices, explained straight

We build wardrobe carcasses in ISI-grade plywood as standard, moving to moisture-resistant or BWP grades where a wall backs onto a bathroom or takes monsoon-facing exposure. Shutters can be plywood or MDF depending on the finish you choose — laminate for durability and the widest design range, acrylic for high gloss, veneer with polish for a natural wood look. Hinges and channels are branded, soft-close, and named in your quotation so you know exactly what's being fitted.

We'll tell you honestly where spending more helps (hardware, carcass grade) and where it mostly doesn't (the most expensive laminate catalogue looks much like the mid one on a finished shutter).

## What the work looks like

The process follows our standard rhythm: free site visit and measurement, design and material selection, itemised quote, manufacturing at our Palanpur workshop, then installation by our own team. A regular wardrobe is typically installed 10-14 days after you approve the design. Installation day itself is mostly assembly and alignment — levelling shutters, adjusting soft-close tension, sealing against the wall line — and we don't consider the job done until doors sit flush and close quietly.

## One wardrobe or eighty

Alongside individual homes, we build wardrobes in volume for builders and contractors — the same design repeated across a tower's worth of flats, delivered floor by floor against a site schedule. If you're comparing quotes for a bulk wardrobe package, ask us for a sample-unit price; it's the cleanest way to compare quality like for like.
