Between online classes, board-exam years and work-from-home that never fully went away, the desk has quietly become one of the most-used surfaces in a Surat home — and in most homes it's still a dining chair pulled up to whatever table was free. A proper study unit costs less than most furniture people buy for looks, and it pays back in posture, focus and a room that finally has a place for the books.

We build study tables and work desks into rooms across Surat — kids' corners, exam-year setups and full WFH stations — each one measured to its wall.

## Built into the room, not squeezed into it

A freestanding desk claims floor space most bedrooms don't have. A built-in unit borrows space the room wasn't using: the alcove beside the wardrobe, the strip under the window (daylight where it helps most), the dead corner two walls make. The desk surface spans wall to wall at exactly the depth the spot allows, storage goes up instead of out — shelves overhead, drawers below — and the floor stays walkable.

For kids sharing a room, we build twin setups with honest separation: two surfaces, two shelf territories, two task lights. Anyone who grew up sharing a desk knows why.

## The details that make a desk work

**Height and depth first.** 29-30 inches high, 22-24 deep, sized to the user — a unit for a nine-year-old and one for a working adult are different furniture. **Wiring, planned.** Grommets in the top, a cable tray beneath, cutouts at the wall's actual plug points; a WFH desk gets monitor-arm reinforcement where needed. **Light, positioned.** A shelf-mounted strip or wall point above the surface, planned so the writing hand doesn't shadow the page. **Storage that sorts itself.** Open shelves for the daily books, closed cabinets for the clutter, a drawer that locks for the phone during study hours — parents ask, we build it.

Surfaces take scratch-resistant laminates that survive compass points and spilled tea; edges are rounded where kids sit.
