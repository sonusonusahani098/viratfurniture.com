Every Surat home has the same complaint in different words: not enough storage. The suitcases live on top of the wardrobe under a bedsheet, the good crockery has no cupboard worthy of it, thirty pairs of family footwear pile at the door, and the "store room" — if there is one — is a place things go to be lost. None of this needs a bigger flat. It needs storage built for the things being stored, in the spaces already going to waste.

Purpose-built storage is quiet work — nobody photographs a loft — but it changes how a home functions more than any showpiece. It's a steady part of what our workshop builds.

## The spaces every flat wastes

Measured honestly, most flats hide 40-60 square feet of storage that ready-made furniture can never use, because it comes in the wrong widths. Our regular rescues:

- **Above-door and passage lofts** — luggage and seasonal storage overhead, with shutters that vanish into the wall tone
- **Under-window seats** with lift-up storage inside
- **The fridge gap and other awkward slivers** — pull-out pantry towers as narrow as nine inches
- **Balcony corners** — moisture-resistant utility cabinets for tools, cleaning stock and the things currently in a plastic drum
- **Blank passage walls** — shallow 8-10 inch cabinets that swallow linen and medicines without pinching the corridor

## Cabinets with one job, done properly

Generic cupboards store everything badly. Purpose-built ones store one thing well:

**Crockery units** get glass-fronted display for the good sets, closed storage for daily steel, drawer inserts for cutlery, and plate racks that end the stack-and-pray system. **Shoe cabinets** get ventilated shutters, angled shelves sized to actual footwear (men's 11s and kids' sandals don't share dimensions), and a top that works as an entry console. **Store-room systems** turn the junk room into an inventory — open adjustable shelving, labelled zones, heavy items low, a strip for the ironing board and ladder. **Utility cabinets** near wash areas hold detergents and buckets in materials that shrug off splashes.

Each of these is measured around what your family actually owns — we've counted saree stacks and Diwali-decoration boxes on site visits, and the furniture is better for it.

## Built to the same standard as the showpieces

Storage lives a harder life than display furniture — heavier loads, rougher handling, damper corners. Carcasses go in ISI-grade ply with moisture-resistant grades where the location demands, shelves are thicknessed and supported for real loads (a shelf of books is heavier than people think), and hinges and channels are branded, because a storage unit opened ten times a day earns good hardware. Finishes match your existing furniture so additions don't look added.

## Quick projects, big difference

Storage work is fast: most single cabinets go from measurement to installed inside 7-10 days, store-room systems in about two weeks. It's also the most phaseable work we do — many families add a unit or two a year, same finish each time, until the home simply has no overflow left. The itemised quote makes phasing easy to plan.
